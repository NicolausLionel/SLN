### Important interactions
I asked Claude to turn the robot characters in our videos into the faces from the photos I had given before, and to write the script for the videos so we could record the voice-over. Claude re-rendered the videos so each robot's head shows a member's face and its body is the colour of their outfit, and created SLN_Video_VoiceOver_Scripts.docx with two speakers per video (01 Intro to AI, 02 Types & How AI Works, 03 Applications, Benefits & Limits, 04 Ethics & Final Challenge). Every line has a start time and a speaker (e.g. “00:10 B · 曾瑞林”), plus [PAUSE] lines and a pronunciation guide for names and terms such as ELIZA, Dartmouth and Weizenbaum.

### Verification and judgment
I watched each finished MP4 to check that every face was matched to the right member and that the files (17–22 MB) were under the 250 MB upload limit. I checked the facts in the script, such as ELIZA and the 1956 Dartmouth workshop, against our study guide chapters. Some members' names were written with the wrong Chinese characters in the script, so I corrected them, and I shortened lines that were too long for the speech bubbles.

### Outcome and learning
The videos now feel personal and every member has a speaking part. I learned that AI can save a lot of editing time, but timing, names and facts can only be checked by watching the real output, and that a script with timestamps makes recording the voice-over much easier.
